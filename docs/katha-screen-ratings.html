Website

apps/web — the public Next.js site, and the home page in its second job as a landing page.

Home

5/10 16 points

Server-rendered rails with a real hero, honest empty/error states and hreflang-correct metadata give it a solid catalogue skeleton. What holds it down is that it is only a catalogue wall: no merchandising hierarchy, no first-session hook, no acquisition copy, and no path to Shorts/Browse/Rewards at 360px.

  1. W1The hero uses banner_url || cover_url inside a lg:aspect-[21/9] frame — a 9:16 portrait cover cropped to 21:9 shows a chin and a shoulder; require banner_url for the hero and fall back to a blurred-cover + centred poster composition instead of object-cover on a portrait.
  2. W2The hero never plays anything; ReelShort and DramaBox autoplay a muted 6–10s hook clip behind the title — mount a muted <video> for the active slide after requestIdleCallback and keep the image as poster.
  3. W3"Watch now" links to ?ep=1 even for a returning viewer with continue_episode_number set; make it "Resume Ep 7" when the server knows a resume point and keep "Start from Ep 1" as the secondary.
  4. W4Hero metadata is a 12px muted line ({episode_count} episodes) — promote it to a 14px gold row reading "60 episodes · 3 free · Ends today" so the offer is legible before the click.
  5. W5There is no numbered "Top 10 in India this week" rail; a ranked rail with 72px outline numerals is the single highest-CTR row on every competitor and costs one rail key from the API.
  6. W6SeriesCard shows only episode count and Free/VIP — add "NEW EP" / "TRENDING" / "COMPLETE" corner ribbons driven by updated_at/is_featured so scanning 40 identical portraits has any signal.
  7. W7Every rail renders at identical weight (text-lg font-semibold, 7 cards, same card width); make the top two rails 1.25× card size or full-bleed so the page has a reading order instead of a wall.
  8. W8PersonalRails fires a second /v1/home call in the browser for signed-in users and shows a 6-card skeleton first — the Continue Watching rail therefore lands ~600ms late and shoves every rail down; render it from the server on a dynamic variant or reserve its height.
  9. W9Guests get no continue-watching at all — persist last-watched {slug, ep, position} to localStorage on progress and render a "Pick up where you left off" rail for anonymous visitors; this is the cheapest D1 retention lever available.
  10. W10There are no genre/mood chips on home (CategoryBar only exists on /series), so a first-time visitor cannot self-select taste; put a scrollable chip row directly under the hero.
  11. W11No rail-impression or card-click events are tracked (track only fires app_open/series_view), so merchandising cannot be measured or ordered by conversion — add rail_impression and card_click with {rail_key, position}.
  12. W12The revalidate = 60 anonymous cache plus per-language rails means the same order for everyone; /config exposes experiment variants that nothing in apps/web reads — wire rail ordering to a variant so this page can be optimised at all.
  13. W13loading.tsx renders hero + 2 rails while the real page usually renders 5–7, so the skeleton→content transition is a visible jump; render 4 rail skeletons and match the card width exactly.
  14. W14The home page emits webSiteJsonLd but no og:image anywhere in the root metadata — a WhatsApp share of katha.app (the dominant Indian share channel) renders a bare link; add an opengraph-image.tsx at 1200×630.
  15. W15Nothing on this page tells a logged-out visitor there is a signup bonus, coin economy, or app — see the landing-page item below.
  16. W16At 360px the hero is aspect-[3/4] = ~480px tall before a single card is visible; drop to aspect-[4/5] on mobile so one rail peeks above the fold.

No dedicated marketing landing page

3/10 11 points

Making home double as the acquisition surface is defensible for a catalogue product and the SSR/SEO plumbing is genuinely good. It fails as acquisition because there is not one sentence of positioning, one proof point, or one conversion CTA above a 32px header button.

  1. W1A logged-out visitor never reads the words "short drama", "free episodes", or "in Hindi" — the only H1-adjacent text is a rail title; add a one-line H1 band above the hero: "Bite-sized dramas in Hindi and English — first 3 episodes free."
  2. W2The only signup CTA on the entire page is the size="sm" (h-8, 12px) "Sign in" button in the header; a real landing surface needs a 48px filled accent CTA with the incentive on it ("Get 100 free coins").
  3. W3The signup bonus exists in the product (ledger.signup_bonus) and is never advertised anywhere in the web app — surface "New users get N coins free" in the hero, the auth dialog and the unlock dialog.
  4. W4There is no explanation of the coin economy for a cold visitor: no "coins → episodes" diagram, no "₹99 ≈ 25 episodes" anchor, no "coins never expire" reassurance outside the wallet subtitle.
  5. W5No social proof: no download count, no ratings, no "10 lakh viewers this month", no press/partner logos — trust is the barrier for an Indian paid-content first purchase.
  6. W6No app store badges anywhere despite com.mobirizer.katha existing; add Play/App Store badges plus a smart app banner so web traffic funnels to the higher-LTV app.
  7. W7No FAQ block (Is it free? What are coins? Can I watch offline? How do I get a refund?) — this is also the cheapest long-tail SEO on the site.
  8. W8No email/WhatsApp capture for visitors who will not sign up today; a single "Get new-episode alerts on WhatsApp" field would out-perform the whole footer.
  9. W9No campaign landing variants (/watch/[campaign]) and no UTM-aware hero, so paid acquisition cannot be tested against anything.
  10. W10robots.ts and sitemap.ts cover /, /series, /shorts and series pages but no category pages and no marketing pages — a landing page would also add the missing /category/* entries to the sitemap.
  11. W11Recommendation: build / as hero-with-clip + value strip (3 icons) + "How coins work" + Top 10 rail + testimonials + FAQ + app badges, and move the raw catalogue to /series, keeping the current home for returning/authenticated sessions.

Browse (/series)

6/10 12 points

Grouping the whole catalogue into per-category rails with server-rendered crawlable links is the right structure and the "See all" affordance is correct. It loses points on request cost, zero filtering, and a layout that shows only ~7 items per category on a page that exists to show everything.

  1. W1The page fires 1 + N requests (fetchSeriesList per category) on every revalidation; a single /v1/series?group_by=category or one limit=100 call filtered client-side would cut TTFB materially — log it in docs/frontend-gaps.md and ask for it.
  2. W2Rails cap at PER_CATEGORY = 12 and the viewport shows 2–7, so "browse all" surfaces a fraction of the catalogue; use a 2-row grid per category with a "See all (48)" count, not a single scroll row.
  3. W3There is no sort control (Newest / Most watched / Free first / Shortest) and no filters (free-only, completed, episode count) — the two things a browse page exists for.
  4. W4Category names carry no counts; "Romance" vs "Romance (128)" changes click-through and sets expectations.
  5. W5browse.subtitle reads "Pick a category, or scroll the whole catalogue" — fine English, but there is no 150-word indexable intro paragraph, so this page ranks for nothing.
  6. W6No loading.tsx for this route: navigating from home leaves the previous page frozen while N category calls resolve; add a skeleton mirroring the rail layout.
  7. W7The uncategorised rail is titled "More on Katha" and silently caps at whatever the limit: 100 call returned — it is unpaginated and unbounded content masquerading as a rail.
  8. W8The failure mode is all-or-nothing: failed requires !all.ok && filled.length === 0; a partially failed set silently drops categories with no "some categories could not load" notice.
  9. W9CategoryBar at 360px is an unmasked horizontal scroller with no gradient fade or arrow — add a right-edge fade so users know there are more than four categories.
  10. W10No BreadcrumbList or CollectionPage JSON-LD here despite this being the main hub page for crawlers.
  11. W11Every supported language renders every series even when untranslated, so /hi/series can be near-duplicate English content under a Hindi hreflang — gate the alternates on actual translation coverage.
  12. W12There is no "A–Z" or alphabetical index, which is the standard deep-link farm for catalogue SEO.

Category

6/10 11 points

Real pagination, breadcrumbs, rel=prev/next on the links and a priority-loaded first row make this the most technically competent catalogue page. It is undermined by a canonical bug, no sorting, and an empty-state that assumes the user is lost.

  1. W1generateMetadata never reads ?offset, so page 2, 3, 4 all emit canonical: /category/slug — deep pages are declared duplicates of page 1 and drop out of the index; append the offset to the canonical.
  2. W2rel="prev"/rel="next" sit on <Link> elements in the body, not in <head>; Google ignores body-level pagination hints.
  3. W3The pager shows "Page 3" with no total ("Page 3 of 7") because hasNext is inferred from items.length === PAGE_SIZE; ask the API for a total count rather than guessing.
  4. W4No sort or filter controls at all, on a page whose entire job is a filtered list.
  5. W5The H1 is the bare category name ("Romance") with no descriptive subhead; "Romance short dramas — 128 series, new episodes weekly" both reads better and ranks.
  6. W6PAGE_SIZE = 40 with 2 columns at 360px is 20 rows of scrolling before any pagination control appears — add a sticky "Load more" or infinite scroll on mobile and keep the numbered pager for crawlers.
  7. W7The offset > 0 empty state ("No more dramas on this page / Go back a page") is a dead end that should never be reachable; clamp offset server-side to the last valid page and redirect.
  8. W8Cards drop the is_premium/free_episodes badge emphasis in grid mode — in a 6-up grid the 11px badge is illegible; scale badge type with the card.
  9. W9No category hero image or colour treatment; every category page is visually identical, so there is nothing to share or remember.
  10. W10priority={i < 6} preloads six images but the grid shows 2 at 360px — over-fetching on the exact devices with the worst bandwidth; make it i < 4.
  11. W11Breadcrumb is text-only with no BreadcrumbList structured data, so rich results show a bare URL.

Series detail

6/10 17 points

The layout is right (portrait player left, info and episode grid right), the lock-state model is faithfully implemented, and the grant-keying by ${status}:${id} is genuinely elegant. It scores mid because the page is an information page where it needs to be a conversion page: no bundles, no VIP upsell, no auto-unlock, no episode context.

  1. W1There is no "Unlock all remaining episodes for N coins" bundle — this is the single largest revenue omission versus ReelShort, where the series-bundle is the dominant ARPU driver.
  2. W2No auto-unlock toggle ("Automatically unlock the next episode with coins"); the reference Android app had it, ReelShort defaults it on, and it converts binge sessions into spend without a dialog per episode.
  3. W3VIP exists in the wallet but is never mentioned on this page or in the paywall — add "VIP unlocks every episode · from ₹X/month" beside the coin price.
  4. W4The episode grid renders bare numbers in aspect-square tiles: at 360px, 5 columns of ~56px squares with an 10px coin price. Give each tile a thumbnail on ≥sm, and on mobile switch to a horizontal thumbnail strip with title + duration.
  5. W5An 80-episode series renders 80 tiles with no pagination, no range selector (1–50 / 51–100), and no "jump to episode" input — the reference paginated at 15/page for a reason.
  6. W6Nothing shows how far the paywall is: add "You have 3 free episodes · Episode 4 costs 30 coins" as a persistent line above the grid.
  7. W7syncUrl uses history.replaceState, so the browser Back button leaves the series entirely instead of stepping back an episode; use pushState for explicit selections.
  8. W8Like and favourite are not optimistic — the heart only fills after the round-trip, which on 3G reads as a broken button; flip local state first and revert on error.
  9. W9"Report" sits in the same button row as Like / My List / Share at equal weight; demote it to an overflow "⋯" menu — it is a support action, not an engagement action.
  10. W10There is no rating, release year, completion status ("Ongoing · new episodes Fridays"), duration-per-episode, or cast; the metadata line is views/likes only, which is the least persuasive data available.
  11. W11On mobile the order is player → title → synopsis → buttons → episodes; the unlock CTA is two screens down. Add a sticky bottom bar: "Unlock Ep 4 · 30 coins · Balance 12" with the CTA.
  12. W12The synopsis "Show more" threshold is a hard-coded length > 180 while the clamp is line-clamp-3 — at 360px three lines is ~120 chars, so the toggle is missing exactly when it is needed.
  13. W13share() sends window.location.href including ?ep=7, so a shared link drops a new viewer into a locked mid-series episode; share the canonical series URL with a ?utm_source=share and the referral code.
  14. W14No BreadcrumbList JSON-LD and no hasPart episode list in the TVSeries payload — episode-level rich results are left on the table.
  15. W15openGraph.images uses the 720×1280 portrait cover with twitter:card = summary_large_image; Twitter/WhatsApp centre-crop that to an unreadable band. Generate a 1200×630 composite (cover + title + "60 episodes").
  16. W16The similar-series rail sits outside the main container with mt-6 and no section separator, reading as an orphan rather than a "keep watching" hook; move it below the episode grid inside the info column on desktop.
  17. W17The signed-in refetch (hydratedFor) replaces the whole episode array after mount, so lock badges visibly flip for authenticated users a second after paint.

Player (embedded in series detail)

6/10 17 points

Solid engineering: hls.js with native-Safari detection, expiry-aware auto-reload, real QoE beacons, subtitle persistence, inert controls and keyboard shortcuts. It scores mid because the control surface is a desktop video player pasted into a vertical short-drama product, and the embed path silently loses everything.

  1. W1There are no ±10s skip buttons in the control bar — only ArrowLeft/Right at ±5s on keyboard; add visible 10s buttons and double-tap-to-seek zones, which is the expected gesture in this category.
  2. W2Hold-right gives 2×, but there is no hold-left rewind (the reference had it) and no speed menu at all — add a 0.75/1/1.25/1.5/2 menu next to the CC button.
  3. W3No quality selector; hls.js auto-ABR is the only option, and the reference product exposed a quality dropdown. On Indian 3G a manual "Data saver / 360p" toggle is a retention feature, not a nicety.
  4. W4No picture-in-picture button and no autoplay-next toggle; onEnded silently advances, which is right, but the user cannot turn it off.
  5. W5The next episode is never pre-warmed — the brief asks mobile to call /play for next_episode_id, and the reference pre-fetched 5 MB of episode N+1. Web does nothing, so every episode boundary is a cold start.
  6. W6The volume slider is hidden lg:block, so on tablets there is only a mute toggle; that is acceptable, but the mute button is 20px in a 30px hit box — below the 44px target.
  7. W7The seek input is a 20px-tall k-range over a 4px track with a 14px thumb; at 360px, scrubbing a 90-second episode is a coin flip. Increase the touch target to 44px and add a drag-preview time bubble.
  8. W8No scrubbing thumbnails (BIF/sprite) and no chapter markers for the cliffhanger — both are cheap ways to make a 60-second episode feel navigable.
  9. W9When embed_html is present the component returns an <iframe srcDoc> early: no custom controls, no progress reporting, no beacons, no next-episode, no resume. A whole class of content is invisible to analytics and continue-watching — at minimum post a synthetic play_start and a periodic progress ping.
  10. W10labels.networkError is "Playback stopped. Tap to reload." shown next to a button on desktop; use "Playback stopped — try again" and reserve "tap" for touch.
  11. W11The buffering spinner has no aria-live announcement and the error overlay is not focused, so a screen-reader user gets silence when playback dies.
  12. W12poster is episode.thumbnail_url ?? series.cover_url, but there is no blurred-fill behind object-contain, so a non-9:16 asset shows black pillarboxes inside a bordered rounded frame.
  13. W13On iOS the fallback is webkitEnterFullscreen, which hands control to the native player and drops the custom UI, subtitles selection and 2× hold — worth a one-line note or a different fullscreen strategy.
  14. W14Keyboard shortcuts (space/k/m/f/c/n) exist but are undiscoverable; add a ? overlay or a tooltip listing them, and expose them via aria-keyshortcuts on the region.
  15. W15preload="metadata" plus no startPosition warm-up means resume seeks after manifest parse — visible jump; pass startPosition (already done for hls.js) and also set currentTime on the native path before first paint.
  16. W16The centre play button (p-5 accent circle) overlays the tap surface and duplicates tap-to-play; on a phone this is two competing affordances — keep the button only while paused-and-controls-visible.
  17. W17No "Episode 4 of 60" chip or progress-through-series indicator inside the player frame, so the viewer never sees how much is left to buy.

Shorts feed

4/10 16 points

Correctly limits itself to one live HLS session with an IntersectionObserver and a clean poster fallback — the engineering restraint is right. The score is low because the feed does not implement the loop the format exists for: it is a carousel of first episodes, not a bingeable vertical feed.

  1. W1Each item plays only first_episode_id; the entire ReelShort/DramaBox mechanic is swipe = next episode of the same series until the paywall. As built, the feed can never convert — it can only send you to the series page.
  2. W2The feed is finite and small: built from three home rails (featured, top_picks, newest), deduped, with no pagination — a user reaches the end in under two minutes and hits nothing.
  3. W3No preloading: the next item's /play grant is requested only after it becomes active, so every swipe is a poster + spinner. Prefetch the grant for index + 1 and mount a second <video> with preload="auto".
  4. W4liked and saved initialise to useState(false) regardless of server state, so a series the user already favourited shows an empty star and a tap silently removes it — this is a correctness bug in a conversion surface.
  5. W5Inactive items render a bg-black/50 play glyph over a 60%-opacity poster, which reads as "broken/unloaded" rather than "paused"; show the poster at full opacity with a title overlay instead.
  6. W6Default muted with the unmute control buried as the top item of the right rail — add a one-time centre tap-to-unmute prompt on the first item, as every competitor does.
  7. W7No progress bar, no elapsed time and no seek; a 90-second episode with no scrub is unusable for rewatching the hook.
  8. W8The container is h-[calc(100svh-3.5rem)], so the site header eats 56px of an immersive surface; hide the header on this route (or make it overlay/transparent) and go full-bleed.
  9. W9No swipe affordance on first load — add a one-shot bouncing chevron + "Swipe up for the next drama".
  10. W10No keyboard support (Arrow Up/Down / PageDown) and the container has aria-label but no role="feed"/aria-posinset, so this screen is unusable without a touch surface.
  11. W11Individual shorts are not addressable (/shorts?s=slug), so nothing here is shareable or deep-linkable — the share button copies the series URL, losing the exact hook that made them share.
  12. W12No shorts_swipe, shorts_watch_depth or shorts_exit events; you cannot tell whether this surface works.
  13. W13An AgeGateDialog is rendered per item, so a 40-item feed mounts 40 <dialog> elements; hoist it to the feed level.
  14. W14The action rail sits at bottom-24 and the title block has pe-14 clearance — on a 360×640 device the "Watch full" button and the rail collide with the toast layer at bottom-4.
  15. W15There is no lock/price preview on the card: "Ep 1 free · 59 more" would convert the swipe into an intent to buy.
  16. W16Metadata declares shorts.description = "Swipe through the first episode of every drama" — honest, but it advertises the limitation; either fix the loop or don't index the page.

Search

5/10 12 points

The results page is server-rendered with a keyed analytics beacon and correct noindex, and the header combobox debounces at 250ms with stale-response keying — both good. It is held back by zero query assistance, no filters, and an accessibility contract the combobox does not honour.

  1. W1No recent searches, no trending queries and no suggested genres on the empty state — "Type something to search" is a blank wall on the one screen with the highest intent.
  2. W2No filters or sort on the results page (free-only, category, episode count, newest) despite the endpoint supporting category.
  3. W3No result count ("18 dramas match 'revenge'"), so users cannot tell a thin result from a broken one.
  4. W4No transliteration or fuzzy matching guidance: Indian users type "kahani", "pyaar", "इश्क़" interchangeably; at minimum add a "Did you mean" and index both scripts server-side (log it as a gap).
  5. W5The dropdown declares role="combobox" with aria-controls/aria-expanded but never sets aria-activedescendant and has no Arrow/Enter navigation over the list — screen readers announce a combobox that cannot be operated.
  6. W6No Ctrl/Cmd+K shortcut to focus search; the reference product had it and it is free to add.
  7. W7Zero-result queries fire the search beacon with results: 0 (good) but nothing surfaces those to the user as "Request this drama" — a catalogue-gap capture form converts a dead end into product input.
  8. W8The results grid reuses SeriesCard with no relevance signal — no matched-term highlighting, no category grouping, no "closest match" ordering explanation.
  9. W9On the results page there is no search input at all: users must scroll back to the header to refine, and on mobile the header input is behind a toggle.
  10. W10The dropdown's "See all results for '{term}'" is a <button> inside a <li> at the end of the list, not a link — it cannot be middle-clicked or opened in a new tab.
  11. W11There is no loading skeleton on the results route (/search has no loading.tsx), so submitting a query freezes the previous results until the server responds.
  12. W12The 60vh dropdown at 360px covers the whole viewport with no scroll shadow or "8 of 40" indicator.

Wallet

6/10 18 points

The purchase state machine is the best-built flow in the app: gateway allow-listing, discount-confirm step, webhook polling with generation guards, and field-level coupon errors. It scores 6 because the pricing psychology is absent — the packs are a neutral price list with no anchoring, no equivalence, and no return path from the paywall.

  1. W1Arriving from the unlock dialog loses all context: UnlockDialog links to /wallet with no ?next= and no episode reference, so after paying the user lands on the wallet with no way back to the episode they wanted. Fix this first — it is the largest single conversion leak in the app.
  2. W2No coin→episode equivalence anywhere: every pack should read "500 coins ≈ 16 episodes" under the coin count; the coin economy is otherwise unpriceable by the user.
  3. W3No price anchoring: no strike-through "was ₹299", no "₹0.40 per episode", no per-coin unit price to compare packs; every tile is an isolated number.
  4. W4No "Most popular" / "Best value" default emphasis unless the API happens to send badge — the middle pack should be visually 1.15× scale with a gold border by default.
  5. W5country: "*" is hard-coded in both the packs call and the checkout body, so India-specific pricing, currency and gateway routing can never be applied; resolve country from the request or /config.
  6. W6Razorpay is labelled just "Razorpay" in a subtle pill; Indian users buy on UPI trust — show UPI / GPay / Paytm / card logos and default the gateway to Razorpay when currency === "INR" rather than gateways[0].
  7. W7formatMoney uses maximumFractionDigits: 2, producing "₹99.00"; Indian pricing convention is "₹99" — set minimumFractionDigits: 0 when the amount is integral.
  8. W8VIP is a read-only status card ("Not active — VIP unlocks every episode") with no CTA; the VIP packs are buried in the same grid as coin packs. Split into two tabs, "Coins" and "VIP", and make the VIP card the entry point.
  9. W9The coupon field renders even when there are no offers, teaching users to leave and hunt for codes — hide it behind a "Have a code?" disclosure.
  10. W10No urgency on the packs themselves: no "first purchase +50% bonus", no expiring first-time offer, no countdown next to a pack — the only countdown lives in the offers panel.
  11. W11Selecting an offer does not restate the discounted price on the pack tiles; the user only learns the new price after clicking through to the confirm dialog. Recompute and show "₹299 → ₹209" inline.
  12. W12No trust row under the packs: no padlock, no "Payments secured by Razorpay/Stripe", no refund policy link, no GST/company line — required both for conversion and for Indian payment compliance.
  13. W13bonus_coins is shown as "+120 bonus" in small success-green text next to a 30px gold coin number; make the bonus a badge on the tile ("+25% extra coins") since it is the actual differentiator.
  14. W14The paid state shows "+500 coins" and a check icon with no celebration motion; a 400ms coin-count-up and a confetti burst measurably lifts repeat purchase.
  15. W15On success the dialog offers only "Done" — it should offer "Unlock Episode 4 now" and take the user straight back to playback.
  16. W16Polling stops at 60s into a "pending" state with a "Check again" button, but nothing tells the user their coins will arrive even if they close the tab; the copy says it, the visual hierarchy does not.
  17. W17loading={busy} is passed to every pack button, so clicking one pack puts a spinner on all three — scope busy state to the clicked pack id.
  18. W18RequireAuth's generic skeleton (h-8 / h-32 / h-32) plus the page-level Suspense fallback={<Skeleton className="h-64" />} means the wallet flashes two different unrelated loading shapes before content.

Wallet history

5/10 11 points

A correct, honest ledger with translated transaction kinds, running balance and cursor pagination. It scores low because it is a desktop data table shipped to a phone-first audience with no filtering and no receipts.

  1. W1The table is min-w-[560px] inside an overflow-x-auto — at 360px the primary column (amount) is off-screen and every row requires horizontal scrolling; render a stacked card list below sm with date + type on one line and the delta right-aligned.
  2. W2No filters by type (purchases / unlocks / rewards) and no date range, so finding "what did I pay on the 3rd" means scrolling 50 rows at a time.
  3. W3No month grouping or sticky date headers; a heavy user's ledger is an undifferentiated wall.
  4. W4Purchases have no receipt or invoice link — Indian users expect a downloadable GST invoice for a paid transaction, and support tickets multiply without one.
  5. W5note ?? ref_type leaks raw internal identifiers (e.g. "purchase", "episode") into a user-facing column; map every ref_type to human copy or leave the cell blank.
  6. W6"Load more" pages on before = last.created_at, which will skip or duplicate rows when two entries share a timestamp — use an opaque cursor.
  7. W7No summary header: "Spent this month: 340 coins · Earned: 120" would make this page worth visiting rather than only worth auditing.
  8. W8No empty-state CTA button — the hint mentions the daily check-in but does not link to /rewards.
  9. W9The delta column colours negatives with text-danger (a pale pink) which on the near-black ground reads as an error rather than a spend; use muted ink for spends and success only for credits.
  10. W10No export (CSV) and no "report a problem with this transaction" link into the contact form with the transaction id prefilled.
  11. W11Page title is robots: noindex and correct, but there is no back-link on mobile other than the ghost "Wallet" button in the header row, which is size="sm" at 12px.

Rewards

6/10 12 points

The 7-day streak strip with aria-current="step", the task timer, and the flag-gated rewarded-ad task are all correctly built, and the streak visual is the most product-y thing on the site. It is capped by weak urgency, a missing referral loop, and no watch-based earning.

  1. W1There is no referral programme surfaced here despite user.referral_code existing — "Invite a friend, both get 100 coins" with a WhatsApp share button belongs at the top of this page, and it is the cheapest growth loop available in India.
  2. W2No loss framing on the streak: "Check in today or your 5-day streak resets" with the day-7 jackpot highlighted converts far better than "Day 5 of 7".

Toasts

6/10 8 points

  2. A2Hovering does not pause the dismissal timer, so a long error you are mid-way through reading vanishes under the cursor.
  3. A3Toasts are bottom-right while the action that failed is usually top-of-form — put save/validation errors inline next to the control, and keep toasts for confirmations.

6/10 8 points

5/10 10 points

  7. M7Icon takes color but the coin branch ignores it, so a coin on a gold button is invisible.
  8. Monetisation psychology

3/10 16 points

  1. X1VIP dominates every coin pack and nothing tells the user. access.py:90 grants access to everything for VIPs; VIP Monthly is ₹299 for 30 days, while ₹799 buys 1,200 coins = 24 episodes.   2. X2The VIP card renders "🪙 0" in 3xl gold. WalletView.tsx:390 prints {p.coins} for every pack, and vip_30 is seeded with coins=0 — the subscription product literally advertises zero coins as its headline number.
  3. X3Price anchoring has no top anchor: the ladder is ₹99 / ₹449 / ₹799 and stops. Without a ₹1,999 "mega" tier, ₹799 is the anchor, and the mid pack cannot be made to look like the sensible choice.
  4. X4The card hierarchy is inverted — coins are the 3xl gold headline and the rupee price is the button label (WalletView.tsx:399). Users decide in rupees. The CTA also has no verb: it reads "₹449", not "Get 550 coins · ₹449".
  5. X5No per-unit math is shown anywhere: no "₹33/episode", no "24 episodes", no strikethrough, no "Save 33%". The bonus is rendered as a flat "+200 bonus" (WalletView.tsx:384) rather than "+20% extra", which is the framing that converts.

  7. X7₹99 is a high entry price for this market with no micro-tier below it. UPI makes ₹19 and ₹29 transactions frictionless in India; the pack ladder ignores the single biggest structural advantage of selling here.

  15. X15There is no price experimentation surface in the client despite the variant map being recorded on every ledger and purchase row — the infrastructure for paywall experiments exists and no client reads a variant to change a price, a free-episode count or an offer.

Competitive positioning

3/10 14 points

Katha has three assets ReelShort and DramaBox do not — AI subtitles and per-language content translation, a real multi-language content pipeline built for India rather than localised into it, and an honest, server-enforced coin ledger; what holds it down is that none of those three appear anywhere in the product's surface, so today Katha is a well-engineered clone of the category's shape with none of its energy and none of its own claim.

  1. X1The one durable Indian advantage is language depth, and it is currently unshipped: seven languages seeded, one populated, four with no font, and rail titles hardcoded in English. Ship Tamil, Telugu and Bengali properly and Katha owns a market ReelShort serves badly.
  2. X2AI subtitles per episode (services/ai/katha_ai/graphs/subtitles.py) are a genuine differentiator in a country where people watch on mute in public — and there is no badge on a card, no "captions in 7 languages" claim, no marketing surface for it.
  3. X3Semantic search and embedding-based similar-series are built and invisible; competitors do title-contains. This is a "why does Katha always find the next one I want" story that is currently told to no one.
  4. X4Do not copy ReelShort's Chinese-melodrama-dubbed catalogue. Katha's economics only work with original vertical Hindi/Tamil/Telugu drama — the content factory in docs/katha-world-class-plan.html is the right bet; the product surface should advertise "made here", not hide it.
  5. X5Do not copy the aggressive interstitial ad load; default_ad_unlocks_per_day = 5 is already too generous and an ad-heavy first session is what makes DramaBox's retention curve fall off a cliff in India.
  6. X6Do copy — immediately — the free-episode countdown, the day-7 jackpot check-in, and the ₹1 first-purchase offer. All three are cheap, all three are proven in this exact market, and none exist here.
  7. X7The guest-playable shorts feed on both web and mobile is a real edge over the predecessor and over ReelShort's Android build; it is not surfaced as a proposition anywhere ("start watching, no signup").
  8. X8Web is a strategic asset that is being wasted: India has enormous mobile-web-first behaviour, and Katha's web app is a full player with HLS, subtitles and guest playback — but there is no PWA, no install prompt, no landing page, and no episode-level URLs to rank on.
  9. X9The category leaders win the paid-social loop with a creative pipeline (dozens of 9:16 cutdowns per series per week) and a deep link that drops the user into the exact clip. Katha has one deep-linkable path, no OG images, and no episode links — it cannot run that playbook at all today.
  10. X10The honest ledger, refund reversals and per-user signed media are a real trust story in a category with a bad reputation for taking money and losing coins — "your coins never expire, your unlocks are permanent" should be on the wallet, the paywall and the store listing. It appears once, on web, in 12px muted text.
  11. X11Kuku FM's India push wins on audio-first, commute-shaped consumption; Katha has AI dubbing on the roadmap and a subtitle graph already producing timed translated text — an audio mode is a defensible flank neither ReelShort nor DramaBox is positioned to take.
  12. X12Regional pricing tables and UPI via Razorpay are built; competitors price in USD-converted tiers. Katha could price at ₹19/₹29 micro-tiers and win the first purchase outright — and currently starts at ₹99.
  13. X13The parity matrix frames the whole strategy as "match SnapReels" — 41 same, 48 better, 10 partial. That is a correctness scoreboard, not a positioning. Nothing in the repo answers "what is Katha the only app to do".
  14. X14The competitive gap is not features. On features Katha already beats its reference product on 48 of 124 rows. The gap is that the product has no voice, no motion, no notifications and no share loop — four things that cost far less than what has already been built.

Cross-cutting summary

Summary 10 points

Weighted overall: 3.4/10. Katha is an unusually well-engineered platform wearing a placeholder brand. The backend is close to category-leading — an append-only coin ledger with idempotency keys, per-user 15-minute media grants with an nginx verifier that production refuses to boot without, webhook-settled purchases with refund reversal, server-enforced sequential unlock, regional price tables, offers and coupons priced server-side, AI translation and subtitle graphs, role-scoped admin, structured logging and a real CI gate. That work is worth a great deal and none of the criticism below touches it. What is missing is everything that sits between that engine and a human being: there is no logo, no motion system, no notification system, no marketing page, no share card, no working share link, no translation data, and no Indic fonts on mobile. Weighting by business impact — acquisition, retention and monetisation psychology carry roughly half the score, brand and craft the rest — the product lands in the low threes because a user's entire experience of Katha today is a competent catalogue that never surprises them, never rewards them, never messages them, and cannot be shared. The distance to a category leader is not architectural; it is a quarter of focused product-and-brand work on top of a foundation that is already better than the thing it was built to replace. The 10 things that most separate Katha from a category leader, ordered by how much they change the business:

  1. X1There is no push notification system. notify.py logs "push.todo", the Notification table is an orphan, Session.push_token is never written, and expo-notifications isn't a dependency. Without it there is no D7, no D30, no streak recovery, no episode-drop alert — retention is left entirely to luck.
  2. X2VIP at ₹299/month grants unlimited access and cannibalises the entire coin ladder (access.py:90 vs ₹799 for 24 episodes), and the VIP card advertises "🪙 0" because WalletView.tsx:390 prints p.coins for a pack seeded with zero. The pricing architecture needs rebuilding before scale, not after.
  3. X3Every link shared from the app 404s on the web. Mobile shares katha.app/s/{slug}; the web has no /s/ route, so proxy.ts rewrites it into the catch-all. The organic loop is not weak — it is broken.
  4. X4The translation pipeline has no data. seed_translations.py reads apps/web/src/i18n/en.json, which does not exist; web and mobile use different key namespaces (301 vs 57 keys, 16 shared, with conflicting values); mobile bundles zero Indic fonts; Tamil, Telugu, Bengali and Arabic have no webfont. The product's core claim — "in your language" — is currently untrue.
  5. X5There is no acquisition surface. No landing page, no /download, no PWA, no OG image generation, no install-referrer, no UTM capture, no episode-level URLs, and a referral system that pays only one side and has no link to share. Paid and organic growth both have nowhere to land.
  6. X6There is no motion. Reanimated ships and is used in one file; the web app has 12 transitions and two keyframes; the mobile skeleton doesn't animate; the coin balance doesn't count; the unlock has no celebration. In a category sold on feel, this is the difference between "an app" and "the app".
  7. X7The paywall is a transaction, not a moment. No countdown to the free-episode wall, no offer at the point of intent, no exact-fit micro-pack when funds are short, no first-purchase discount, no ₹19–₹49 tier, and auto-unlock buried below the fold in untranslated English.
  8. X8The identity is a placeholder and the team knows it — "real icons" is a tranche-2 line item. A flat pink K on near-black loses the Play Store thumbnail against ReelShort and DramaBox before a single install decision is made, and there is no logo asset, feature graphic or screenshot set in the repo.
  9. X9Content ratings are invisible to viewers despite being added to the API for exactly that purpose, the age gate is a single forever-tap with no DOB and no parental lock, and unrated series fail open. That is regulatory exposure under India's IT Rules and a store-review risk, and it is a two-day fix.
  10. X10The funnel is unmeasurable. The event allowlist has no install, onboarding_complete, first_play, checkin, notification_open or referral_share — so none of the nine problems above can be sized, prioritised or proven fixed from the data the product currently collects.
